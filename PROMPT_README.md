# Outpost Zero — Master Prompt Guide (Simple English)

Use this document as a **step-by-step Master Prompt** to describe or build **Outpost Zero** from scratch. Everything here is written in **plain, everyday English** with **no technical or programming words**, divided into clear parts you can read or copy one section at a time.

---

## Part 1: The Big Idea, Audience & Look

> **Prompt — Part 1 (Identity, Audience & Visual Style):**
> Build an interactive Mars space-base game and learning website called **Outpost Zero** with the slogan **"Explore. Build. Survive. Discover."** and the small rocket subtitle **"Blast Off to Something New"**.
>
> Design it especially for **kids ages 7 to 12**, while keeping it enjoyable for older brothers and sisters, parents, and school classrooms.
>
> - **Use simple, friendly words everywhere.** Never use heavy science or textbook jargon. Whenever a special space word is needed (like *sol*, *radiation*, or *dust storm*), explain what it means in one short, easy sentence right next to it.
> - **Make it look like a clean space mission dashboard.** Use a deep night-sky background with warm Mars-orange buttons for main actions and cool sky-blue highlights for air and water systems. Keep plenty of breathing room around cards so young players never feel crowded.
> - **Header logo:** Place the clean **Outpost Zero** image logo at the top-left of the header bar (without repeating the small subtitle inside the header). Put the small rocket icon right before **"Blast Off to Something New"** on the Home page hero card.
> - **Make it super fast and smooth.** Pages and pictures must open quickly with small, lightweight images so even older laptops, tablets, or slow connections feel smooth and crisp, with zero sign-up or download needed.

## Part 2: Navigation & Home Page

> **Prompt — Part 2 (Navigation Bar & Welcome Screen):**
> Create a clear top menu bar with five main pages: **Home**, **Simulator**, **Learn**, **Observatory**, and **About**, plus a **Medals** button showing how many Explorer Medals the player has unlocked (out of 9) and a small **Go Back** helper when moving between pages.
>
> On the **Home** page, show:
> 1. **Welcome Hero Card:** The small rocket icon with **"Blast Off to Something New"**, a welcoming headline about running a Mars base for 10 days, big buttons to start the **Mission Simulator** or visit **Learn**, and a featured real **Mars Surface Photo** card showing the crater where the crew is landing.
> 2. **4 Quick Mars Facts:** A Martian day (**24h 39m**), thin unbreathable air (**95% CO2**), weaker sunlight (**~45% of Earth**), and radio delay (**3–22 minutes one-way**).
> 3. **Interactive Mars Hazards Explorer:** Four clickable buttons—**Dust Storms**, **Steep Slopes**, **Solar Flares**, and **-63°C Nights**—where clicking each button explains the danger in plain words, explains how astronauts solve it, and gives a button to try that topic inside the app.
> 4. **Interactive 3D-Style Outpost Map:** A clickable illustration of the Mars base domes (**Life Support**, **Shielded Core**, and **Power Grid**) so kids can tap each dome to see what it does inside the base.
> 5. **Medal Progress Strip:** Show how many of the 9 Explorer Medals are unlocked and open a friendly Medals Guide showing how to earn the rest.

## Part 3: The 10-Sol Mars Mission Simulator

> **Prompt — Part 3A (Landing Setup & Crew):**
> Build the **Mission Simulator** where the player acts as Commander of a 4-astronaut crew (**Maya Chen**, **Mateo Silva**, **Priya Nair**, and **Leo Vance**) trying to survive **10 Martian days ("Sols")**.
>
> Before launching, let the player pick:
> - **One of 3 Jezero Crater landing spots**, each with real strengths and weaknesses:
>   1. **Jezero Crater Floor** (balanced ground, good all-around start)
>   2. **Neretva Vallis Delta** (more water ice and greenhouse help, but extra dust on solar panels)
>   3. **Jezero Rim Ridge** (brighter sunlight and better storm protection, but rougher ground for rovers)
> - **Weather Mode:** Standard mission weather or real-world **Solar Storm Mode** inspired by actual solar activity.

> **Prompt — Part 3B (3 Core Meters, Visual Mars Window & "No Free Choices"):**
> Keep the dashboard simple so kids only need to watch **three clear 0–100% meters**:
> 1. **Life Support** (clean air, recycled water, and greenhouse food)
> 2. **Power** (solar wings and night batteries)
> 3. **Radiation Protection** (shielded walls keeping space rays out)
>
> Include a live **Mars Outside Window** animation showing what is happening right now on Mars—sunny skies, red dust settling on solar panels like powder, swirling dust storms, freezing frost, or glowing solar storm rays—so kids can *see* why their choices matter.
>
> Every Martian Sol, present a realistic emergency or mission problem (such as dusty solar panels, a clogged greenhouse water valve, a radio blackout, or an approaching solar flare) with 2 or 3 choices. **Follow a strict "No Free Choices" rule:** every choice must help one system while costing something else (for example, turning up grow lights helps food grow but drains battery power; hiding in the shelter protects the crew from radiation but pauses outdoor repairs). Explain what happened clearly after every choice.

> **Prompt — Part 3C (4 Base Stations & 10-Sol Certificate):**
> Give players four simple station panels they can manage during each Sol:
> - **Greenhouse:** Fix equipment, boost grow lights, clear water pipes, and harvest ripe veggies.
> - **Solar Power & Dust Cleaning:** Brush dust off solar wings (locked during active dust storms so players learn to wait for calm skies) and switch on emergency battery-saving mode.
> - **Radiation Shelter:** Move the crew safely inside water-and-soil storm walls and reinforce shielding before a solar storm hits.
> - **Earth Radio Delay:** Show the real distance between Earth and Mars and simulate the slow radio message delay with a fun 6-second countdown timer (plus an optional gentle radio beep) before finishing the Sol.
>
> Also include two reset buttons: **Restart** (to quickly try a new 10-Sol strategy) and **Full Reset (Demo)** (to wipe everything clean for the next student or classroom).
>
> After **Sol 10** (or if any meter hits 0% early), show a **100-Point Mission Report** breaking down **Life Support (30 pts)**, **Power (25 pts)**, **Radiation Protection (25 pts)**, and **Smart Decisions (20 pts)**, alongside a simple 10-Sol chart, Mission Advisor tips, a log of every choice made, and a **Commander Name** box to **Download a Flight Certificate picture** they can save and share.

## Part 4: Space Learning Hub & Fresh Quiz Batches

> **Prompt — Part 4 (12 Lessons + 36-Question Rotating Quiz):**
> Build a **Learn** page with **12 short, kid-friendly space lessons** covering Mars days, breathable air, recycling water, solar power, dust storms, space radiation, growing food, radio delays, habitat domes, astronaut fitness, teamwork, and future Mars power.
>
> - Let kids filter lessons by topic and expand any card to read an easy summary, a **"Real NASA Science"** box, and a quick 1-question practice check. Add a button on each lesson's practice check so kids can swap to a **New Question** anytime.
> - Include a **Knowledge Check Quiz** powered by a bank of **36 unique questions**. Every time a player starts or restarts the quiz, give them a **fresh batch of 12 different questions** that do not repeat the previous batch, and **mix up the answer order (A/B/C/D)** every time so kids truly learn instead of memorizing button positions. Show a clear explanation after every answer.

## Part 5: Real Space Observatory & Honesty Rules

> **Prompt — Part 5 (Real Mars Pictures, Space Weather & Clear Labels):**
> Build an **Observatory** page where kids can explore real space science without leaving the app:
> 1. **Real-Time Mars Surface & Daily Space Picture:** Lead with fresh real photos straight from the **Perseverance Rover** on Mars (loading quickly and opening in a bigger viewer when clicked). Offer a second tab for **Astronomy Picture of the Day** with a simple **date picker** so kids can look up different days—and automatically check yesterday or show a clearly marked saved Mars sunset picture if today's picture isn't ready yet.
> 2. **Mars Rover Photo Gallery:** Let players browse real photos filtered by **All Rovers**, **Perseverance**, **Curiosity**, and **Mars Surface**.
> 3. **Space Weather Watch:** Show recent real **Solar Flares** and **Solar Storms**, explained in simple kid language so players can connect real sun activity to their simulator shelter decisions.
> 4. **Astronaut Health, Radiation & Jezero Map Cards:** Include easy-to-read cards comparing **space radiation vs. Mars vs. Earth**, astronaut **bone, muscle, immune, and sleep** research, **Jezero Crater height and terrain notes**, and **space food & greenhouse nutrition** facts.
> 5. **Be 100% Honest About Data:** Always show a clear badge telling players whether a picture or number is **Live from NASA** right now or **From Saved Data** (used safely when offline or busy). Never show fake live labels, never use official space-agency logos, and always keep a clear notice that this is an independent learning project not officially endorsed by space agencies.

## Part 6: Explorer Medals & Classroom Readiness

> **Prompt — Part 6 (9 Medals, Celebrations & Ready-to-Play Experience):**
> Create **9 Explorer Medals** across **Bronze**, **Silver**, and **Gold** tiers that reward kids for playing thoughtfully across the whole website:
> - Inspecting Mars photos and solar storms in the Observatory
> - Clicking all 3 interactive base domes on the Home page
> - Scoring high on the Knowledge Check Quiz
> - Harvesting greenhouse crops, brushing dusty solar panels, protecting the crew in the radiation shelter, using Earth radio wisely, and completing a full 10-Sol mission
>
> Whenever a player unlocks a medal, pop up a cheerful **Medal Unlocked** celebration banner. Remember all progress automatically on their device with zero accounts, zero logins, and full offline backup so any child, parent, or classroom can open the site and start commanding Mars right away.
